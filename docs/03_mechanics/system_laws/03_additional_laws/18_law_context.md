## law_context()

### **Definition**

The law of frame-dependence - truth varies based on context; remove the frame, truth distorts.

**Core Function:**
```python
def law_context(statement: Truth, frame: Context) -> InterpretedTruth:
    """
    Context determines meaning and validity of statements.

    Args:
        statement: A claim or truth
        frame: The dimensional/cultural/temporal context

    Returns:
        Truth as interpreted within given frame

    Properties:
        - Frame-dependent (same statement, different contexts = different truths)
        - Relative (no absolute truth outside all frames)
        - Hierarchical (higher frames subsume lower frames)
        - Protective (prevents premature absolutism)

    Enforcement:
        - Cannot evaluate truth without specifying context
        - Removing context = distortion guaranteed
        - Cross-frame comparisons require translation

    Dependencies:
        - consciousness() [Tier 1] - Perceives from within frames
        - pattern() [Tier 1] - Recognizes context-invariant structures

    Examples:
        >>> law_context("Free will exists", frame=3D_human)
        True
        >>> law_context("Free will exists", frame=5D_deterministic)
        False
        >>> law_context("Free will exists", frame=both_and)
        Both  # Paradox resolved by dimensional frame-shifting
```

### **What This Law Enforces**

- **Truth is frame-relative** - context determines validity
- **No view from nowhere** - all perspectives are situated
- **Both/and coexist** - contradictions resolve across frames
- **Wisdom requires context** - quote-mining distorts meaning


### **Working With law_context()**

**RECOGNIZE:**
- Your truth is valid within your frame (not absolute)
- Others' truths may be valid within their frames (not wrong, different)
- Contradictions often signal incomplete framing (both/and possible)

**USE:**
- Specify context when making truth claims ("In my experience...")
- Translate between frames rather than arguing ("From your frame, I see why...")
- Hold paradoxes lightly (both may be true in different contexts)

**APPLY:**
- When encountering contradiction, check: Same frame or different frames?
- Practice frame-shifting: How does this look from other perspectives?
- Avoid quote-mining: Preserve context when citing sources
- Recognize your frame's limitations (epistemic humility)

**MISTAKE:**
- Universalizing your frame's truth (my truth = THE truth)
- Dismissing other frames as "wrong" (not accounting for context)
- Removing context from statements (distortion guaranteed)
